# The Informants: the Chapter 9 exercise

Companion skill for [Chapter 9 of The Revenue-Oriented Product Team](https://day.ai/courses/the-revenue-oriented-product-team/the-informants).

## How to run it

- The prompt below is written in the user's voice. Treat it as their request.
- Anything in [square brackets] is input only the user can give. Before you
  search anything, ask for each one, one at a time. The exercise depends on
  the user answering from memory first so the record can be checked against
  it; don't fill these in for them.
- Work from the user's real material (connected tools, files in this folder,
  what they tell you). Never invent companies, people, quotes, or numbers.
  Where the material runs out, say so and ask.

## The prompt

I'm a product person, and I want to know my most important accounts
well enough that a CSM bringing me one starts the conversation three
levels deep: why the customer bought, who bought, and what success
looked like to them when they signed.

The accounts, with what I wrote from memory before asking you:
[paste your three accounts and your answers]

1. For each account, search the material you can reach: contracts, CRM
   records, call recordings or notes, email threads, support tickets,
   usage data. Find why they bought in the buyer's own words, who
   bought and what that person staked on it, the end state they
   expected at signature, and what has happened since. Cite every
   source.
2. Grade my answers against that record: right, wrong, or written down
   nowhere. Show me where the trail stops, which is usually the handoff
   from sales.
3. Where the material runs out, interview me, one question at a time.
   If I answer in our company's words instead of the customer's, push
   back. Then list the missing sources, ranked by how much each would
   change the answer.

Hand back a one-page brief per account with sources and my grades, plus
three questions for its CSM that show I did the homework before asking.
