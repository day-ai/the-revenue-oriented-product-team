# The Two Species of Companies: the Chapter 1 exercise

Companion skill for [Chapter 1 of The Revenue-Oriented Product Team](https://day.ai/courses/the-revenue-oriented-product-team/the-two-species-of-companies).

## How to run it

- The prompt below is written in the user's voice. Treat it as their request.
- Anything in [square brackets] is input only the user can give. Before you
  search anything, ask for each one, one at a time. The exercise depends on
  the user answering from memory first so the record can be checked against
  it; don't fill these in for them.
- Work from the user's real material (connected tools, files in this folder,
  what they tell you). Never invent companies, people, quotes, or numbers.
  Where the material runs out, say so and ask.

## The prompt

I'm working out whether my company is sales-driven or product-driven.
Definitions: in a product-driven company, problems get solved in the
product by default and every function participates productively in the
product conversation; in a sales-driven company, the scoreboard
functions own the revenue conversation and product is a service
provider to it.

First, ask me to briefly state my own opinion on whether we are product-driven
or sales-driven and why.

Then, we should pressure test my take based on real data.
Using everything available in this environment (e.g. notes, meeting transcripts,
exported docs, tickets, strategy files), assemble the evidence for each
side. I want behaviors, not stated values: who attends what, who decides
what, what happened in specific recent cases where a customer problem
could have been fixed in product.

Where we don't have enough information to answer, interview me one question at a time.
Try to push me towards sharing specific facts and stories rather than generalizations.

At the end, we should have two artifacts:
1. A one-page assessment of where we fall on product-driven to sales-driven that
   I could show a peer without flinching, with the evidence cited.
2. A raw material list: the source material this environment is missing
   that would have made this assessment trivial (call recordings? support
   tickets? the financial plan?), ranked by how much each would change
   the answer.
