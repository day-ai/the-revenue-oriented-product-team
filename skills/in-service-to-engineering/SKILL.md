# In Service to Engineering: the Chapter 2 exercise

Companion skill for [Chapter 2 of The Revenue-Oriented Product Team](https://day.ai/courses/the-revenue-oriented-product-team/in-service-to-engineering).

## How to run it

- The prompt below is written in the user's voice. Treat it as their request.
- Anything in [square brackets] is input only the user can give. Before you
  search anything, ask for each one, one at a time. The exercise depends on
  the user answering from memory first so the record can be checked against
  it; don't fill these in for them.
- Work from the user's real material (connected tools, files in this folder,
  what they tell you). Never invent companies, people, quotes, or numbers.
  Where the material runs out, say so and ask.

## The prompt

I want to see how customer impact reaches the engineers on my team and
where the credit goes missing. Engineers are motivated by impact and by
the respect of their peers, and product can build the paths that carry
a customer's reaction back to the person who built it.

My last five releases, from memory, with who got credit and whether the
customer's reaction reached the builder:
[paste your list]

1. From the material you can reach (release notes, the product tracker,
   Slack, support tickets, call recordings, customer email), rebuild the
   last five things my team shipped. For each, find who was told, who
   was thanked by name, what customers said, and whether the engineer
   ever saw it. Link what you read.
2. Compare that with my list: where my memory was right, where it was
   generous, what I missed. Where the record is thin, interview me, one
   question at a time. Push back if I count an announcement nobody
   answered as credit.
3. Suggest three ways, using tools we already have, to put a customer's
   reaction in front of the builder within a day of shipping, easiest
   first.

Hand back a five-row table (release, builder, credit given, customer
reaction, whether it reached the builder) and the one change you'd make
first.
