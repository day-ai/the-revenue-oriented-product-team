# The Renewal Clock: the Chapter 10 exercise

Companion skill for [Chapter 10 of The Revenue-Oriented Product Team](https://day.ai/courses/the-revenue-oriented-product-team/the-renewal-clock).

## How to run it

- The prompt below is written in the user's voice. Treat it as their request.
- Anything in [square brackets] is input only the user can give. Before you
  search anything, ask for each one, one at a time. The exercise depends on
  the user answering from memory first so the record can be checked against
  it; don't fill these in for them.
- Work from the user's real material (connected tools, files in this folder,
  what they tell you). Never invent companies, people, quotes, or numbers.
  Where the material runs out, say so and ask.

## The prompt

I'm a product person, and I want to see renewals coming early enough
to help, ninety days out, with the facts my CSMs and engineers need.

My ten most important accounts, with the renewal dates I guessed from
memory:
[paste your list]

1. Find each account's renewal date, cancellation-notice deadline, and
   contract value in the material you can reach: signed contracts and
   order forms, CRM records, billing data. Cite the source for each,
   and mark which of my guesses were right.
2. Where a date is missing, draft a one-line ask to the account's
   owner saying what you checked. If you can't tell who owns it,
   interview me, one question at a time.
3. For the account with the nearest notice deadline, draft a note to
   its CSM: issues we resolved for them, their open requests, the
   usage trend, any change in who champions us. End it with "What did
   we miss?"
4. I'll name one thing I want my team to build. Assemble its story for
   my tech lead: the customers who asked, their contract values and
   renewal dates, and their own words from calls or tickets, quoted
   with where to find them. Push back if the evidence is thin.

Hand back the table sorted by notice deadline, the missing-date asks,
the CSM note, and the engineer story.
